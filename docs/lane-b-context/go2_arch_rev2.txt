1. Decision Record
The team evaluated six candidate architectures (see GO2 AI Architecture — Design Options). The following were selected:
[TABLE]
Selected | Role in the design
Option 1 — Lexical normalisation | Removes orthographic false positives before comparison
Option 2 — Phonetic verification (Rev 2: whole recording) | A phoneme recogniser runs once over the whole recording; every word is checked against its expected phonemes. Finds miscues WhisperX hides and implements the Phil-IRI dialectal-variation rule
Option 6 — Detect-then-diagnose | The structural shape: Options 1 and 2 form the two stages; in Rev 2 the diagnosis stage covers every word
[/TABLE]

Not selected: Option 4 (reference-first) is recorded as future work. Option 5 (constrained decoding) is rejected on validity grounds. Option 3 (parallel dual-lane) is not adopted as a separate design, but Rev 2 takes its central idea — both recognisers process the whole recording — while keeping WhisperX as the single authority on words (see 1.1).
How the three combine. Option 6 is not a separate component. It is the organising structure: a cheap, high-recall detection stage proposes a label for every word from the word lane, and a diagnosis stage confirms, clears or overrules each label using the phonetic lane. Option 1 lives in the detection stage; Option 2 is the diagnosis stage.
1.1 Revision 2 decision — phonetic lane over the whole recording
Problem with Rev 1. WhisperX's decoder is a language model. When a learner mispronounces a word, WhisperX frequently writes the passage word anyway (fink → think). Alignment then reports a match, Rev 1 labels it CORRECT, and the verifier never sees it. Rev 1 could therefore remove WhisperX's false positives but could never find a miscue WhisperX did not flag — recall was bounded by WhisperX itself, which conflicts with FR F4 (all miscues must be assessed accurately).
Decision. Run the phoneme recogniser once over the whole recording, in parallel with WhisperX. Join its output to WhisperX words by timestamp and diagnose every word. WhisperX remains the authority on which word was read, where and when; the phonetic lane answers only how each word sounded.
Accepted costs. A second model in every request (time and roughly 1.2 GB memory for a 300M-parameter recogniser), more logic (join, coverage check, two thresholds), and a new risk: phoneme-recognition slips on correctly read words can create false positives. The risk is controlled by a stricter overrule threshold (5.6), the accent table (6) and the tests in Section 10.
Rejected alternative — phonetic lane only. Phonemes alone give no word boundaries, no timings and no identity for substituted words, and nothing corrects the recogniser's own slips. That design is Option 4 without its scoring model and remains future work (12).

3.1 Alignment cost constraint
Symptom observed: the majority of word positions classify as OMISSION.
OMISSION is the fallback branch of the classification decision tree — it fires when no hypothesis token is aligned to a passage position. Mass omission therefore indicates alignment failure, not classifier failure.
Finding (Rev 2). The current aligner is not Needleman–Wunsch. miscue_classifier.py uses difflib.SequenceMatcher, which has no cost model, so there are no cost constants to correct. Two properties of it produce the symptom:
Greedy anchoring. It locks in the longest run of exactly equal words first and never revisits it; ties go to the earliest passage position. With few exact matches (a struggling reader) and a repeated phrase in the passage, the first anchor can pair the start of the passage with the end of the reading.
Position pairing. Inside a replace block, words are paired by position, so one extra or missing word shifts every pairing after it.
passage: the boy saw the dog and the dog saw the boy
spoken : a boy see a dog an a dog see the boy

SequenceMatcher: 9 insertions, 2 correct, 9 omissions   (anchors on the wrong "the boy")
Needleman-Wunsch: 11 positions in order - 5 match, 6 sub
Action: replace the aligner with the 5.4 design. The cost constraint applies to the new aligner:
REQUIRED:   mismatch_cost  <  2 × gap_cost
With gap_cost = 0.9 and a graded mismatch cost capped at 1.0, delete-plus-insert (1.8) is never cheaper than pairing two words in the same position.
3.2 Insertion detection requires re-verification
Insertion detection was previously recorded as working correctly. Under the failure above, unmatched hypothesis tokens would flood the insertion branch — so apparent insertion activity may be the same defect presenting differently. Re-verify against a recording with a known insertion count. Label the fixture from the audio, not from the transcript: WhisperX drops many inserted fillers and false starts, so a transcript-based count can pass while real insertions are missed.
3.3 Taxonomy corrections
See Section 7. Four corrections are required to conform to the 2018 Phil-IRI manual; they are independent of the architecture and should be scheduled alongside the alignment fix.
3.4 Transcriber output defects (new in Rev 2)
Defects in transcriber.py that reach the classifier before any architecture applies:
Punctuation retained. WhisperX words carry punctuation (dog., said,) while the passage tokenizer strips it. Each sentence end becomes a mismatch that widens replace blocks. Normalisation rule 1 (5.3) must run on ASR words.
Missing timing. Words with no alignable characters (digits, symbols) can lack start, end and score. transcriber.py reads seg["start"] directly and raises KeyError; scoring reads the first and last word's timings.
Missing score treated as certainty. seg.get("score", 1.0) gives an unaligned word the highest possible confidence.
Alignment score used as refusal. score < 0.3 currently means refusal_to_pronounce. The score measures how well WhisperX's own text fits the audio, not whether the learner attempted the word. Removed with Refusal per 7.1.
Action: strip punctuation through the normaliser; mark words without timings as unverifiable and interpolate their times for display only.


4. Architecture Overview
                    +--------------------------------------------+
passage_id -------->|  STAGE 0 - Passage Preparation (cached)    |

                                 + coverage candidates (speech no word covers)
                                                                           |

                      repetition . self-corr     Phil-IRI
Key property 1 — every word is diagnosed. A word WhisperX auto-corrected to the passage word is still compared with what was heard. Rev 1 sent only Substitution and Mispronunciation flags to the verifier.
Key property 2 — WhisperX remains the word authority. It supplies word identity, word boundaries and timings. The phonetic lane only answers how each word sounded. Time is the join key between the lanes.
Key property 3 — overruling WhisperX needs stronger evidence. A word WhisperX matched becomes a miscue only above the stricter T_OVERRULE threshold (5.6, 9).
Key property 4 — graceful degradation. If Lane B is unavailable, the pipeline returns Lane A labels. The request never fails because of the phonetic lane.
5. Component Specification
5.1 Stage 0 — Passage Preparation
Runs once per passage and is cached; not per request. The cache key is passage_id plus a hash of the passage text and the lexicon version, so edits invalidate the cache.
[TABLE]
Output | Source | Notes
Raw token list | passages.passage_text | The scoring denominator. Never derived from normalised units.
Normalised units | Normaliser (5.3) | Comparison keys for the passage side
Expected phoneme sequences | espeak-ng (IPA) → custom lexicon; CMUdict / g2p_en only through an exact ARPAbet→IPA mapping | Same symbol set as the recogniser (5.6). Stress markers stripped. All accepted variants per word (the: ðə / ði)
Proper noun set | passages.proper_nouns, falling back to capitalisation extraction | See 8.2 — the DB column does not yet exist. Capitalisation extraction misses sentence-initial names
[/TABLE]

Custom lexicon requirement. Phil-IRI English passages are Philippine-contextualised and contain Filipino proper nouns and terms (Aling Nena, Cebu, jeepney, pandesal). These are absent from CMUdict, and English-trained G2P applies English orthographic rules to them, producing wrong expected pronunciations and false mispronunciations on correctly-read words.
The lexicon is hand-written, finite (an estimated 30–60 entries across the full passage set), and tractable because Filipino orthography is near one-to-one grapheme-to-phoneme. Multiple accepted variants should be listed where both Filipino and anglicised pronunciations are legitimate readings.
Symbol-set rule (Rev 2). Expected and observed phonemes must use one inventory. The recommended recogniser emits espeak IPA, so generating expected phonemes with espeak-ng avoids a lossy mapping. Heteronyms (read, live, lead) need sentence context or must list both pronunciations as accepted variants.
5.2 Token model
Every token carries two representations:
@dataclass
class Unit:
    surface: str              # original text - never modified
    key: str                  # normalised comparison key
    passage_span: int = 1     # raw passage words this unit covers
    source_indices: list[int]
    start: float | None = None    # hypothesis side only
    end: float | None = None
    align_score: float | None = None    # WhisperX alignment score; None if unaligned
    verifiable: bool = True             # False when WhisperX gave no timing

@dataclass
class WordVerdict:                      # one per passage position, built in 5.6
    expected_phonemes: list[list[str]]  # accepted variants from Stage 0
    heard_phonemes: list[str]           # Lane B phonemes joined by time
    distance_expected: float | None     # best variant vs heard
    distance_hypothesis: float | None   # WhisperX word vs heard
    deviations: list[tuple[str, str]]   # (expected, heard) phoneme pairs
Alignment matches on key. Display, annotated transcript, and scoring use surface. Overwriting surface corrupts Phil-IRI scores silently.
passage_span exists because multi-word contractions merge into single units. A merged unit representing "do not" carries passage_span=2, and scores credit two words when correct.
Hypothesis units without WhisperX timing are marked verifiable = False. The arbiter keeps their Stage 3 label rather than guessing which phonemes belong to them.
5.3 Stage 1 — Lexical Normalisation (Option 1)
[TABLE]
INVARIANT / One function, applied identically to the passage and to the ASR hypothesis, producing the same key for the same sounds. A rule that runs on only one side is a defect.
[/TABLE]

Rule order is fixed and significant:
[TABLE]
# | Rule | Behaviour
1 | Orthographic | Unicode-normalise apostrophes, lowercase, strip punctuation retaining apostrophes. Applies to ASR words too (dog. → dog)
2 | Numbers (new) | Spell digits as words (5 → five; 1990 → nineteen ninety, with listed variants)
3 | Multi-token merge | Fuse known expanded contractions (do not → one unit, key dont); set passage_span on the passage side
4 | Compounds (new) | Hyphenated, spaced and closed forms share one key (ice-cream / ice cream / icecream; every one / everyone where listed)
5 | Contractions | Closed lookup table; canonical form is the collapsed variant
6 | Possessives | Strip trailing ’s / s’; also strip a trailing plural s when the passage word is possessive and the hypothesis is not
7 | Apostrophe removal | Drop remaining apostrophes
8 | Homophones | Map to an equivalence class identifier (there, their, theyre → H_THERE)
9 | Fillers (new) | Remove um, uh, er from the unit list and record them separately; not Phil-IRI miscues (team to confirm)
[/TABLE]

Order dependency at rules 5–6: it’s and boy’s are orthographically indistinguishable. Contractions are a closed set, so that table is consulted first; any remaining ’s is a possessive. Numbers (rule 2) run before merging so that spelled-out numbers can merge like other multi-token units.
Homophone table scoping. English contains thousands of homophones; the system needs only those appearing in its passages. Extract the unique vocabulary across all passages, intersect with a homophone list, and store the result. This yields an estimated 30–60 entries and is complete for the deployed passage set.
Justification. Phil-IRI scores what the learner says. A homophone read aloud is not a miscue, and orthographic variants of the same utterance are not miscues. Treating them as equivalent is semantically correct, not a workaround.
5.4 Stage 2 — Alignment
Needleman–Wunsch over key values, returning a complete operation trace, not a match list.
[("match", 0, 0), ("sub", 1, 1), ("delete", 2, None), ("insert", None, 2)]
Invariant: every passage index and every hypothesis index appears exactly once across all operations. Assert both in tests — an incomplete trace makes the classifier fill gaps with omissions or silently drop insertions.
Costs:
MATCH    = 0.0
GAP      = 0.9
MISMATCH = graded   # 0.0-1.0, proportional to edit distance between keys
Graded mismatch cost means near-misses align as substitutions (the learner attempted the word) while unrelated words prefer a gap. Flat cost cannot express that distinction.
Proper-noun relaxation applies here, not in the normaliser — it is asymmetric (a lexicon built from the passage, loosening matches on the hypothesis) and therefore cannot satisfy the 5.3 invariant:
def sub_cost(p_unit, h_unit, proper_nouns):
    if p_unit.key == h_unit.key:
        return 0.0
    if p_unit.key in proper_nouns and fuzzy_close(p_unit.key, h_unit.key):
        return 0.3
    return min(1.0, levenshtein(p_unit.key, h_unit.key) / max(len(p_unit.key), 1))
Tie-break. When two moves have equal cost, prefer pairing (diagonal) over a gap: assume the learner attempted the word. The rule is fixed in code and covered by a test so identical recordings always produce identical traces.
Graded cost input. When keys differ, compute the graded cost on the form before rule 8. Homophone class identifiers (H_THERE) carry no spelling similarity.
Two-to-one merge. TC-07d-1 (Maria / Mari a) needs one passage unit matched to two hypothesis tokens, which one-to-one alignment cannot produce. Add a merge move: adjacent hypothesis tokens whose concatenated key equals, or proper-noun-fuzzy-matches, a passage key align as one unit.
5.5 Stage 3 — Detection (Option 6, first stage)
Maps alignment operations to provisional labels. Tuned for recall. In Rev 2 every position proceeds to diagnosis; the provisional label decides which threshold applies there.
[TABLE]
Operation | Provisional label | Diagnosis in 5.6
match | CORRECT | Yes — stricter T_OVERRULE (Rev 1: not checked)
sub (real word) | SUBSTITUTION | Yes — T_FLAG
sub (non-word / low similarity) | MISPRONUNCIATION | Yes — T_FLAG
delete | OMISSION | Yes — the gap is checked for speech
insert | INSERTION | To the post-pass (5.7)
(no WhisperX word) speech in a gap | COVERAGE CANDIDATE | To the post-pass (5.7) — new in Rev 2
[/TABLE]

5.6 Stage 4 — Phonetic Lane and Arbitration (Option 2, Option 6 second stage)
Lane B starts as soon as audio.wav exists and runs in parallel with WhisperX; it needs only the audio. Per request:
Recognise — run the phoneme recogniser once over the whole recording, without VAD trimming, to get phonemes with frame timestamps (about 20 ms per frame; repeats merged, blanks dropped).
Join by time — assign each phoneme to the hypothesis unit whose window [start − pad, end + pad] contains it (JOIN_PAD_MS, Section 9). Phonemes in no window form coverage candidates.
Compare — weighted phoneme-sequence edit distance against every expected variant (best variant wins) and against the WhisperX word's phonemes, using the cost matrix in Section 6; normalised by expected length.
Arbitrate — apply the rules below to every word.
Record — store distances and deviating phonemes on every word (WordVerdict), whether or not the label changes. This feeds threshold tuning and abstention (Section 11).
Arbitration rules (step 4), applied per word:
[TABLE]
Stage 3 label | Phonetic evidence | Final label
CORRECT (match) | Distance to the passage word ≤ T_OVERRULE | CORRECT
CORRECT (match) | Distance > T_OVERRULE, deviations outside the accent table | MISPRONUNCIATION — WhisperX auto-corrected
SUBSTITUTION / MISPRONUNCIATION | Distance to the passage word ≤ T_FLAG | CORRECT — WhisperX misheard, or an accent variant
SUBSTITUTION / MISPRONUNCIATION | Closer to the WhisperX word than to the passage word | SUBSTITUTION
SUBSTITUTION / MISPRONUNCIATION | Far from both | MISPRONUNCIATION
OMISSION | No speech phonemes in the gap | OMISSION
OMISSION | Speech in the gap close to the passage word | CORRECT — VAD or WhisperX dropped a spoken word
Any, unit not verifiable | — | Stage 3 label kept
[/TABLE]

Asymmetric thresholds. T_OVERRULE > T_FLAG. WhisperX's language model corrects its own listening errors on correctly read words; the phonetic lane has no such correction and can mishear single phonemes. A word WhisperX matched therefore needs stronger evidence before it becomes a miscue. Both values are tuned against the regression fixture (Section 9).
Why the whole recording, not clips. Rev 1 extracted one clip per flagged word. Forced-alignment boundaries are tight and clip edge consonants, and phoneme models degrade on sub-second input. One pass keeps acoustic context and replaces the Rev 1 per-clip batching requirement.
Graceful degradation. If Lane B is unavailable or fails, the pipeline returns Stage 3 labels with WhisperX timings — equivalent to Option 1 alone — and records that the phonetic check was skipped. This must not fail the request.
5.7 Stage 5 — Sequence Post-Pass
Two Phil-IRI behaviours are not expressible in one-to-one alignment and require a pass over the trace.
Repetition and self-correction. Both present as one passage position with two attempts. In Rev 2 they arrive from three sources: an insertion adjacent to a matched or substituted position (attempts WhisperX kept); a coverage candidate from 5.6 (attempts WhisperX dropped); and a duration anomaly — a unit much longer than its phoneme count predicts (DURATION_RATIO), hiding several attempts inside one WhisperX word:
for each insertion or coverage candidate adjacent to a word position:
    if not phonetically similar to the neighbouring passage word:
        leave as a genuine INSERTION
    elif first attempt matched the passage word:
        REPETITION        -> 1 error;  remove the phantom insertion
    else:
        SELF_CORRECTION   -> 0 errors; remove the phantom insertion
Multi-attempt runs (mon… monk… money) collapse to a single verdict. Partial attempts may not survive ASR as dictionary words — use similarity, not exact match.
Run collapsing. The 2018 manual counts a word or phrase omitted as one error; likewise for insertion and repetition. Consecutive operations of the same type collapse into a single miscue event. Three adjacent omissions are one omission, not three. Two omissions belong to one run when they occupy adjacent passage positions with no spoken unit between them.
Transposition. A swapped pair (A B read as B A) appears either as two adjacent substitutions where each hypothesis word equals the other passage word, or as insert–match–delete around a shared word. Both patterns collapse to one TRANSPOSITION (one error).
Reversal (label only). A substitution whose hypothesis word is the passage word reversed (was / saw), or with adjacent letters swapped, is relabelled REVERSAL. The miscue count is unchanged.
5.8 Stage 6 — Scoring
Oral Reading Score = (number of words - number of miscues) / number of words x 100
number of words is len(passage.raw_tokens) — always the raw list, never normalised units.
Each miscue carries equal weight regardless of type; the 2018 manual applies no major/minor distinction.
A correct merged unit credits passage_span words.


6. The Accent Equivalence Table
6.1 Requirement
ReadRight assesses Filipino Grade 4 learners reading English passages. Philippine English has a systematic phonological profile. A generic English acoustic model scores these features as mispronunciations, producing a false-positive source larger than the enumerated FR-07 defects — systematic rather than random, and therefore it does not average out.
[TABLE]
PHIL-IRI COMPLIANCE / The 2018 Phil-IRI manual states directly, under the mispronunciation scoring rule, that dialectal variation should not be counted as an error. Handling this is a Phil-IRI compliance requirement, not an optional enhancement. A system that flags dialectal variation as a mispronunciation does not implement the standard correctly.
[/TABLE]

6.2 Documented substitutions
[TABLE]
Target phoneme | Realised as | Example
/f/ | /p/ | farmer → parmer
/v/ | /b/ | victor → bictor
/θ/ | /t/ | think → tink
/ð/ | /d/ | that → dat
/z/ | /s/ | zoo → soo
/ʒ/ | /ʃ/ | measure → mesher
[/TABLE]

Additional reported features are encoded explicitly in Rev 2. Matched words are now diagnosed, so any feature missing from the table turns a correctly read word into a miscue:
[TABLE]
Feature | Encoding (low cost) | Example
Tense / lax vowels | /ɪ/↔/i/, /ʊ/↔/u/ | ship → sheep, full → fool
Low front vowel | /æ/→/a/ or /ɛ/ | cat → [kat]
Reduced schwa | /ə/ realised as a full vowel in unstressed syllables | banana → [ba-na-na]
Final cluster simplification | Deleting the last consonant of a word-final cluster | test → tes
[/TABLE]

6.3 Implementation
A version-controlled data file, not inline constants. Pairs listed here receive near-zero substitution cost in the 5.6 distance computation; all other phoneme substitutions retain articulatory-feature-weighted cost.
This replaces Soundex/Metaphone, which the current SDD specifies. Soundex discards vowels almost entirely — and vowel errors are among the most diagnostically significant miscues in Grade 4 readers. Neither Soundex nor Metaphone can express a targeted equivalence relation; a weighted phoneme distance can.
6.4 Open decision
Treating /θ/→/t/ as cost-free means a learner who genuinely cannot produce th is never flagged. For a reading-fluency tool this is likely correct; for a pronunciation tool it would not be. The team must decide deliberately and record the decision in the SDD. Rev 2 raises the stakes: because matched words are diagnosed, every pair left out of the table can make a correctly read word a miscue.
6.5 Regional caveat
Published Philippine English phonology is predominantly Tagalog-influenced. The pilot cohort is in Cebu City, a Cebuano-speaking area. The substitution set is broadly shared, but the team should verify against actual recordings rather than assume identity.
7. Miscue Taxonomy
Per the 2018 Phil-IRI manual, Table 5.
[TABLE]
# | Type | Detection in this architecture | Counted
1 | Mispronunciation | Phonetic lane shows deviation outside the accent table — on any word, including words WhisperX matched (5.6) | Yes
2 | Omission | Alignment delete with no speech in the gap; consecutive runs collapse | Yes
3 | Substitution | Phonetic lane is closer to the WhisperX word than to the passage word | Yes
4 | Insertion | Alignment insert or coverage candidate surviving the 5.7 post-pass | Yes
5 | Repetition | Post-pass: two attempts, first correct — including attempts WhisperX dropped | Yes
6 | Transposition | Post-pass swapped-pair pattern (5.7) | Yes
7 | Reversal | Substitution relabelled by string check (5.7) | Yes
— | Self-correction | Post-pass: two attempts, first incorrect | No
[/TABLE]

7.1 Required corrections to current behaviour
Repetition is an error. The current decision tree marks it non-counting. The 2018 manual counts every repeated word or phrase as one error. This changes every score the system produces.
Self-correction must be detected in order to be excluded. Without it, the system counts an error the manual explicitly does not. Note that enabling this makes average scores rise — that is the fix working, not a regression.
Transposition and Reversal are absent from the current taxonomy.
Refusal to Pronounce should be removed. It is not in the 2018 Table 5, and operationally it denotes the teacher supplying the word — ReadRight has no teacher. Fold into Omission and justify from the single-Learner design.
7.2 Scope decisions on types 6 and 7
Transposition — include. Omitting it causes a swapped pair to score as two errors where the manual specifies one. It overcounts, penalising the learner.
Reversal — labelled in Rev 2. A reversal produces a real but incorrect word, which the detection stage labels SUBSTITUTION. A string check (5.7) now relabels it; the count and the Oral Reading Score are unchanged.
The decision rule applied: a type is mandatory when omitting it changes the miscue count. Labelling accuracy alone is secondary, because the 2018 formula weights all types equally.


8. Integration with readright-api
8.1 New Protocols and wiring
The service is Protocol-based with a single composition root. New components follow the existing pattern — one file per domain in app/models/, holding the model and its *Protocol.
[TABLE]
Component | Protocol | Lifecycle in dependencies.py

GO2Pipeline is assembled in get_go2_pipeline() from these plus the existing transcriber, classifier, scorer, passage repository, reporter, and proper-noun extractor. No router or service imports a concrete class.
The phoneme model must load once at startup, in the same lifespan block as WhisperX and MediaPipe. Per-request loading is not viable. Budget roughly 1.2 GB of memory for a 300M-parameter recogniser in 32-bit floats; check development machines and the VM.
Concurrency. GO2Pipeline starts Lane B when audio.wav exists and joins the lanes after Stage 3. On a GPU the phonetic lane overlaps WhisperX; on a CPU both compete for cores, so measure wall-clock time in the spike (8.4).
8.2 Required database change
passages.proper_nouns is referenced by PassageRepository.fetch but does not exist; the method always returns [], so proper-noun handling relies solely on capitalisation extraction. This directly weakens FR-07d.
Adding the column also gives the custom lexicon (5.1) a home. One migration unblocks both.
8.3 Environment
Each added model increases exposure to the existing Windows FFmpeg/torchcodec fragility. Containerise the AI service before the verifier is integrated, not during. Verify the chosen phoneme model installs and runs on the team’s development machines before committing it to a sprint plan. espeak-ng, used for expected phonemes, is a system dependency like ffmpeg and belongs in the container image.
8.4 Validation spike
Before planning the verifier sprint, run the candidate phoneme model over three existing full recordings and confirm that (a) the phonemes match what is audible, (b) the join by time assigns phonemes to the right words, and (c) speech WhisperX dropped appears as coverage candidates. Record wall-clock time and peak memory for WhisperX alone and for WhisperX + Lane B. A half-day spike establishes whether Rev 2 is buildable on the team's hardware.

All are validated, not chosen. Each requires a test case and a recorded justification in the Software Test Document.
[TABLE]
Parameter | Purpose | Constraint

THE MOST IMPORTANT TEST IN THIS DOCUMENT / Every rule added makes the system more permissive. 10.5 Before/after comparison
Run the previous and new pipelines over the same recordings and diff the labels. Hand-check a sample of changed labels, sorted into false positives correctly removed and real miscues wrongly erased. If the second set is not near-empty, a rule is too permissive.

11. Open Recommendation — Confidence-Gated Abstention
Not included in the team’s selection. Recorded here for an explicit decision.
The pattern introduces a third outcome alongside Correct and Miscue: Uncertain. Words the system cannot classify confidently are excluded from the scored count rather than guessed at.
The argument for inclusion:
ReadRight has a single Learner role and no review step. A false positive reaches a nine-year-old with nothing in between. The original design had teacher review; that check was removed.
Until the accent table is validated against real recordings, abstention is the only safeguard preventing systematic accent-driven false positives from reaching learners as reading failures.
It falls out naturally from 5.6, which in Rev 2 produces distances for every word. Matched words whose distance lies near T_OVERRULE are precisely the uncertain cases.
Reporting accuracy alongside coverage ("94% of positions classified, 96% agreement on those") is a stronger validation statement than a single figure computed over forced guesses.
The argument against: an abstention rate that is too high produces a results summary full of gaps and a tool that feels broken. The acceptable rate is a GO4 presentation question.
If rejected, record in the SDD assumptions that GO2 returns a binary verdict on every word including low-confidence cases, and that accent-driven false positives are unmitigated until Section 6 is validated.
